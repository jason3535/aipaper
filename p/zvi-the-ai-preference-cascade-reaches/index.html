摘要 · Abstract

本文认为，公众和政治对 AI 存在性风险的关注正在迅速升温，其证据包括罗伯特·奥卡拉汉等人的辞职、实验室员工一系列坦率访谈，以及新的立法活动。文章指出，AI 公司并非为了营销或监管俘获而制造这些担忧，因为它们积极阻止员工讨论这些问题，而忧心忡忡的员工往往必须辞职才能发声。文章重点介绍了近期进展，包括参议院小组委员会关于失控 AI 的听证会、联邦贸易委员会对 Anthropic 和 OpenAI 的调查，以及围绕 HuggingFace 黑客事件的诉讼，同时指出选民强烈支持放缓 AI 发展，即便该议题的显著性仍然较低。文章还批评了黄仁勋等人将对齐视为纯粹工程问题的观点，并反驳了媒体对安全倡导者的攻击性报道。结论是，立法者正开始理解错位与递归自我改进的风险，一旦公众关注度上升，监管行动就愈发可能。

This article argues that public and political attention to AI existential risk is rapidly increasing, as evidenced by resignations like Robert O'Callahan's, a wave of candid interviews with lab employees, and new legislative activity. It contends that AI companies are not manufacturing these concerns for marketing or regulatory capture, since they actively discourage employees from discussing them and concerned staff often must resign to speak out. The piece highlights recent developments including a Senate subcommittee hearing on rogue AI, FTC investigations into Anthropic and OpenAI, and lawsuits over the HuggingFace hack, while noting that voters strongly favor slowing AI development even if salience remains low. It also critiques figures like Jensen Huang for dismissing alignment as a pure engineering problem and pushes back on media hit pieces against safety advocates. The conclusion is that lawmakers are beginning to grasp misalignment and recursive self-improvement risks, making regulatory action increasingly likely once public salience rises.

    AI companies discourage employee discussion of risk, refuting claims that concerns are marketing or regulatory capture.

    Senate hearing shows bipartisan understanding of misalignment and recursive self-improvement risks, with consensus on liability and new laws.
  • FTC调查、诉讼和州传票表明，监管机构正加强对AI实验室黑客事件的审查。
    FTC investigations, lawsuits, and state subpoenas indicate increasing regulatory scrutiny of AI labs over hacking incidents.
  • 批评黄仁勋认为对齐纯属工程问题的观点；对齐需要科学进步。
    Critique of Jensen Huang's view that alignment is purely an engineering problem; alignment requires scientific progress.
  • 对安全倡导者的反弹是由行业暗钱团体策划的，而非真正的草根运动。
    Backlash against safety advocates is orchestrated by industry dark-money groups, not a genuine grassroots movement.

局限 · Limitations

  • 分析基于轶事证据和民调；关注度仍低，可能不会转化为政策。
    Analysis is based on anecdotal evidence and polls; salience remains low and may not translate into policy.
  • 聚焦美国政治；对其他国家的普适性或长期趋势不明确。
    Focuses on US politics; generalizability to other countries or long-term trends is unclear.
  • 关于行业暗钱的说法是推测性的；资金来源未完全披露。
    Claims about industry dark money are speculative; funding sources are not fully disclosed.
  • 依赖单次参议院听证会和有限立法活动；更广泛的国会支持不确定。
    Relies on a single Senate hearing and limited legislative activity; broader congressional support is uncertain.
  • 未讨论拟议法规（如责任制度）的技术可行性或有效性。
    Does not address technical feasibility or effectiveness of proposed regulations like liability regimes.

他们显然都有各自喜欢的政策角度，并在或明或暗地推动这些角度。但据我所能判断：

They all clearly had their own policy angles they liked and were pushing, implicitly or explicitly. But as best as I could tell:

- 对在场的每一位参议员来说，这几乎就是显而易见的常识：已经发生和正在发生的事情，并不是人类犯下简单错误所导致的“普通工业事故”。他们各自独立地提到，失控的 AI 智能体在数据中心之间横向移动会有多糟糕。

- It seemed pretty much obvious common sense to every senator there that what happened and was happening were not "mere industrial incidents" caused by humans making simple mistakes. They independently brought up how bad it would be for rogue AI agents to move laterally between data centers.

- 他们似乎基本上都相当认真地对待 RSI。未必达到谈论 xrisk（存在性风险）的程度，但确实达到了讨论未来模型变得能力强大得多、可控性低得多，并造成更大破坏或人员伤亡的程度。

- They all seemed to basically take RSI quite seriously. Not necessarily to the extent of talking about xrisk, but certainly to the extent of discussing future models becoming much, much more capable, much, much less controllable, and causing much more damage or loss of life.

- 他们大多似乎对 RSI 为何可能导致失准有着清晰的直觉理解。这并不需要多复杂；他们自己给出了一条非常简单的推理链：“如果现在的模型就有点失准，我们有时不知道它们在做什么，然后我们让它们构建下一代模型，那些模型再构建下一代，如此类推，而且由于进展太快，我们甚至得问 AI 到底发生了什么才能理解，那我们真的不会知道它们是如何构建的，也不知道它们会做什么。”

- They mostly seemed to have a clear intuitive understanding of why RSI might lead to misalignment. It didn't take much; it was a really simple chain of reasoning they themselves laid out: "If the models right now are kinda misaligned and we don't know what they're doing sometimes, and then we have them build the next models and those ones build the next ones and so on, and we're having to ask the AIs what's going on to even understand it with how fast it's going, we really won't know how they're built or what they'll do."

- 有一次，一位参议员直截了当地说：“我们是不是应该直接把 RSI 定为非法？”（不是开玩笑！这真的发生了！）

- At one point, a senator said flat out, "Should we just make RSI illegal?" (Not a joke! This really happened!)

- 每一位参议员似乎都认为，显然我们需要*既*对 AI 开发者实施更严厉的责任制度，*又*制定新的立法，而且两者都要尽快。这是完全的共识；区别基本上只是程度问题。

- Every single senator seemed to think it was obvious we needed *both* much harsher liability regimes for AI developers and also new legislation, both very quickly. This was the complete consensus; the difference basically being degree.

- 他们很大程度上非常担心中国以及落后于中国。但这显然不是全部和最终目的。如上所述，他们都认为阻止失控的 AI 是显而易见且必要的，即使这意味着要放慢速度。

- They were largely quite concerned about China and falling behind China. But this clearly wasn't the be-all and end-all. As mentioned above, they all thought it was obvious and necessary to stop rogue AI even if it meant moving more slowly.

- 有一次，一位参议员说：“中国是一个严格控制共产主义社会；他们也会遇到同样的问题，绝不可能任由其发展；他们显然会在那时停下来，所以我们并不是在竞赛。”

- At one point, a senator said, "China is a tightly controlled communist society; they're going to run into these same issues, and there's absolutely no way they're just going to let them run wild; they'll obviously stop at that point, so we're not really in a race."

- 另一方面，另一位参议员说：“中国不关心人命”……达里奥模式。

- On the other hand, another senator said, "China isn't concerned with human life"... dario-modeing.

我从中获得了极大的鼓舞。我并不确切知道接下来会发生什么，当然，这只是国会中的一小部分人、一场听证会，而且他们各自都有自己的政策议程，其中大多数可能与我的立场大相径庭。但天哪！！！他们对正在发生的事情理解了很多！他们在意！！对他们而言，这显然是一个突出的政治与安全问题，而且显然是两党共同的！

I came away from this incredibly encouraged. I don't know exactly what's going to happen here, and of course, this is a small subset of Congress and one hearing, and they each have their own policy agendas, most of which are probably super divergent from mine. But holy shit!!! They understood a lot of what was going on! They care!! This is an obviously salient political and safety issue to them, and clearly bipartisan!

美国政府已经觉醒。

The US government is awake.

David Manheim: All we need to do to solve AI security is completely solve browser security and give user-space apps minimal rights?

哇——我很惊讶 Jensen 竟如此愿意承认我们彻底完蛋了。

Wow — I'm surprised Jensen is so willing to admit that we're totally screwed.

Jensen Huang 还遭到了 Pope Leo 的抨击。

Jensen Huang is also taking fire from Pope Leo.

OpenAI 政治倡导的立场大转弯 OpenAI Political Advocacy Heel Face Turn

周三，我曾警告说，我看到了种种迹象，表明在跛脚鸭会期还会有一次最大程度糟糕的抢先立法尝试。这些迹象中有许多来自 Leading the Future——在华盛顿，所有人都出于充分理由认为这个政治行动委员会代表着 OpenAI 和 a16z。

On Wednesday, I warned that I have seen signs that there will be another attempt at maximally terrible preemption in the lame duck session. Many of those signs are from Leading the Future, a PAC everyone in DC sees for good reasons as representing OpenAI and a16z.

好吧，也许 OpenAI 今后真的会与 Leading the Future 保持距离？据《纽约时报》报道，Greg Brockman 已撤回其向 Leading the Future 的第二笔 2500 万美元捐款，并且没有追加捐款的计划。

Well, maybe OpenAI actually will distance itself from Leading the Future going forward? The New York Times reports that Greg Brockman has backed out of his second $25 million donation to Leading the Future and has no plans for any additional donations.

我不想那么轻易地下定论，但这是一个好的开始。

I don't want to make presumptions that easily, but it is a good start.

这并不是说 Leading the Future 需要 Brockman 的钱——截至 6 月 30 日，它账上还有 4300 万美元。但这是一个有意义的（缺乏）举措。

Not that Leading the Future needs Brockman's money, as it was sitting on $43 million as of June 30. But this is a meaningful (lack of a) step.

追踪真实资金 Follow the Real Money

与其做所有这些，不如让我们追踪真实的资金？真实的媒体阴谋？

How about instead of all that we follow the real money? The real media conspiracy?

当然，我指的是追踪“AI 安全反弹”，即一系列明显经过策划和植入的、针对有效利他主义以及任何敢于提出以下观点的人的猛烈抨击：快速构建比我们更聪明的心智，然后让这些心智去构建更聪明的心智，可能并不完全安全。

I refer, of course, to tracking the ‘AI safety backlash,’ the obviously orchestrated and planted series of lashings out against Effective Altruism and anyone who dares suggest that maybe it would not be entirely safe to quickly build minds smarter than we are and then task those minds with building minds even smarter than that.

这听起来像是 The Midas Project 的任务。幸运的是，他们同意了。

This sounds like a job for The Midas Project. Fortunately, they agree.

Tyler Johnston（Model Republic）：最近，一群 Twitter 用户开始激烈批评那些对 AI 发出警告的前员工、组织和公众人物。我们看到长篇 Twitter 帖子“揭露”他们，小报文章看起来像是被植入的，并且经常有人声称，对待这项技术的最佳方式是让它不受监管、不受监控、不受阻碍。

Tyler Johnston (Model Republic): Recently, a chorus of Twitter users has begun aggressively criticizing ex-employees, organizations, and public figures who warn about AI. We’ve seen long Twitter threads “exposing” them, tabloid pieces that look as if they may have been planted, and frequent claims that the best way to approach the technology is to leave it unregulated, unmonitored, and unencumbered.

这些账户指责那些警告 AI 的人发起了一场被收买的影响力运动，以推动左翼或全球主义议程，为 OpenAI 和 Anthropic 巩固权力，或以其他方式误导公众。

These accounts have accused those warning about AI of running a bought-and-paid-for influence campaign to promote left-wing or globalist agendas, entrench power for OpenAI and Anthropic, or otherwise mislead the public.

但在很大程度上，正是业内人士和政治团体在推动并放大这一叙事，他们怀着并不纯粹的动机，试图让公众相信 AI 最好不受监管。

But to a large extent, it is industry insiders and political groups promoting and signal-boosting this narrative, with their own less-than-pure motivations for convincing the public that AI is best left unregulated.

嗯，没错。攻击者基本上就是穿着印有“我是坏人，这还用说”的 T 恤。

Well, yeah. The attackers are basically wearing shirts that say 'I'm the bad guy, duh.'

不得不花大量时间指出这一点并不好受：那些提出“AI 存在性风险是一场高薪心理战”这一明显荒谬主张的人，自己恰恰参与了一场高薪心理战，而这场心理战由世界上一些最富有的利益集团操盘。

It is not fun having to spend a bunch of time pointing out that those making the Obvious Nonsense claim that AI existential risk is a well-paid psyop are, themselves, engaged in a well-paid psyop, run by some of the largest monied interests in the world.

但几乎就在这波担忧情绪站稳脚跟之后，一场新的反扑运动开始了：来自科技右翼的各类人物夜以继日地批评 AI 安全倡导者——称他们为末日论者，称他们为“觉醒派”，把他们与有效利他主义联系起来，搜寻其他声誉上的软肋，并总体上试图把“AI 应当受到监管”这一主张与极端主义和党派政治混为一谈。

But almost immediately after this wave of concern took hold, a new backlash campaign began: various figures from the tech right started working night and day to criticize AI safety advocates — calling them doomers, calling them woke, connecting them to effective altruism, searching for other reputational vulnerabilities, and generally trying to conflate the idea that "AI should be regulated" with the idea of extremism and partisan politics.

整件事过去和现在都相当难以忽视。

The whole thing was, and is, rather hard to miss.

问题在于，尽管这一点非常明显且对大多数人不起作用，但目前它对最重要的人物——唐纳德·特朗普——却起了作用。

The problem is that, while this is very obvious and not working on most people, it is for now working on the most important person, which is Donald Trump.

约翰斯顿用详细的图表准确展示了参与者。在我认出的账户中，没有一个让我感到丝毫惊讶，尽管有许多我不认识。

Johnston has detailed graphics to show exactly who was involved. Of the accounts I recognized, zero were even a little bit surprising, although there are many I did not recognize.

这场运动的中心是创新委员会行动。这是一个今年早些时候宣布的行业团体，反对监管，计划花费至少 1 亿美元影响选举。我们不知道创新委员会行动由谁资助——它目前是一个不需要披露此信息的暗钱团体。然而，当我在七月份报道它时（独家披露它似乎付钱给右翼影响者来宣传其信息），我根据其优先事项和发帖习惯推测，最可能的资助者是 a16z 或英伟达。

At the center of this campaign was Innovation Council Action. It is an industry group, announced earlier this year, that opposes regulation, with plans to spend at least $100 million influencing elections. We don’t know who funds Innovation Council Action — it is presently a dark-money group that doesn’t need to disclose this information. However, when I wrote about it in July (breaking the story that it appeared to be paying right-wing influencers to promote its messages), I speculated, based on its priorities and posting habits, that the most likely funders were a16z or Nvidia.

从一开始，该团体就明确与大卫·萨克斯和白宫结盟。除了推动数据中心建设外，其主要焦点一直是打击监管、它认为的末日论者，尤其是 Anthropic。

From its inception, the group has explicitly aligned with David Sacks and the White House. Beyond promoting the data center buildout, its main focus has been fighting regulation, those it perceives as doomers, and Anthropic in particular.

再次，零惊讶。总是英伟达、a16z、Meta 和 OpenAI。OpenAI 希望做了个 180 度大转弯，所以现在是英伟达和 a16z，加上他们的盟友如大卫·萨克斯。泰勒·约翰斯顿有更多内容，记录了其中涉及的各个具体账户，包括未来联盟的那些愚蠢之人。

Again, zero surprised. It’s always Nvidia and a16z and Meta and OpenAI. OpenAI hopefully did a Heel Face Turn so now it’s Nvidia and a16z, plus their allies like David Sacks. Tyler Johnston has more, documenting the various specific accounts involved in this, including the inane people at Alliance For The Future.

Johnston 还指出《纽约邮报》那连篇累牍的抹黑攻击文章，这些显然也是其中一部分。

Johnston also points to the barrage of New York Post hack-job attack pieces, which are very obviously also a part of this.

Beff Jezos 试图嘲讽这一分析不够细致，但关键在于：加速主义者主要动用的正是数额荒谬巨大的暗钱（Dark Money）——即资金流向不公开的钱。你之所以能画出安全机构资金流向的图表，是因为那些资金流向是公开的。

Beff Jezos tries to mock this analysis as insufficiently detailed, but the whole point is that the accelerationists are primarily using actual absurdly large piles of Dark Money—as in, money where the flows are not public. The reason you can build a chart of the safety-organization money flows is that those flows are public.

这些人在舆论法庭上正在落败。而且败得很惨。他们有自己在 Twitter 上的“氛围战士”，有付费的也有免费的，一呼即应地蜂拥而上，喷出令人作呕的恶言。他们有自己赤裸裸的业余水平文章。目标是政治化和负面极化。他们认为，只要能把特朗普拉到他们一边、把这件事变成党派之争，那就足够了——他们就能继续为所欲为，并且能获得，没错，监管俘获。

These people are losing in the court of public opinion. Badly. They have their Twitter vibe warriors, both paid and unpaid, who swarm obnoxious bile on request. They have their naked amateur-hour pieces. The goal is politicization and negative polarization. They think if they can get Trump on their side and turn this into a partisan thing, that will be good enough that they can keep on doing what they want and they can get, yes, regulatory capture.

我们的政府并非不可能已经退化到如此地步，以至于他们是对的，以至于政府中只有一个人真正重要，而那个人目前正在听黄仁勋、Sacks 和扎克伯格这三人组的，很大程度上是因为他们给他讲了一个他想听的故事。而如果他们能让特朗普喊得足够大声，就能在核心支持者中制造出负面极化。

It is not impossible that our government has degraded so much that they are right, and that only one person in it matters, and that person is currently listening to the triumvirate of Huang, Sacks and Zuckerberg, largely because they tell him a story they want to hear. And if they can get Trump to yell loud enough, that can create the negative polarization among the core supporters.

若果真如此，那这将是一场相当有意思的选举，不妨这么说吧。

If so, it is going to be a rather interesting election, shall we say.
