我对开源模型与网络风险的看法错了吗？

Was I wrong about open models & cyber risks?

内森·兰伯特 Nathan Lambert · Interconnects · 2026-10-30 · Interconnects ↗

打开互动全文版（逐段中英对照 + 针对本篇提问）→

摘要 · Abstract

本文重新审视了作者此前的论断，即 AI 模型的网络风险在一个被称为“黑色十月”的时期开始显现，其标志是 PHP2Shell 和一处长期存在的 GitLab 漏洞等严重漏洞的披露。核心论点是，围绕开放权重模型的担忧——尤其是对 Claude Mythos 假设性发布的担忧——被夸大了：作者认为，即便 Mythos 以开放权重形式发布，世界大体上也不会有大碍，事件数量的任何增加都更像是加速，而非风险上的阶跃式变化。文章以 GLM-5.3 和 Epoch AI 的 Firefox 漏洞利用等为例，强调网络危害需要多个阶段相互配合才能形成，而威胁行为者更像是有固定动机的企业，而非会转向每一个新 CVE 的机会主义者。作者总结道，当前的转变更多是由 AI 能力及其扩散这一总体趋势所驱动，而非开源与闭源模型之间的分野；网络安全是一个社会性问题，需要 AI 行业之外的努力。

This article revisits the author's earlier claim that AI models' cyber risks began materializing during a period dubbed "Black October," marked by critical vulnerability disclosures such as PHP2Shell and a long-standing GitLab bug. The core argument is that fears surrounding open-weight models, particularly the hypothetical release of Claude Mythos, were exaggerated: the author contends that even if Mythos had been released as open-weight, the world would have been more or less fine, with any increase in incidents resembling an acceleration rather than a step change in risk. Drawing on examples such as GLM-5.3 and an Epoch AI Firefox exploit, the piece emphasizes that cyber harm requires multiple stages to align and that threat actors behave like businesses with fixed motives, not opportunists who pivot to every new CVE. The author concludes that the current transition is driven more by AI capabilities and diffusion generally than by the open-versus-closed model divide, and that cybersecurity is a societal problem requiring effort beyond the AI industry alone.

局限 · Limitations

  • 作者承认并非网络安全专家，预测误差很大且自述感到不安。
    Author admits not being a cyber expert; predictions carry high error bars and acknowledged nervousness.
  • 依赖发布一个月后缺乏公开证据，观察扩散性危害的窗口过短。
    Relies on absence of public evidence after one month, a short window for observing diffuse harms.
  • 将开放权重风险与一般 AI 扩散混同，开放与闭源问题未获充分检验。
    Conflates open-weight risk with general AI diffusion, leaving the open-versus-closed question under-tested.
  • 假设攻击者资源有限且采用开放模型缓慢，这一行为前提未经检验。
    Assumes attackers are resource-limited and slow to adopt open models, an untested behavioral premise.
  • “没事”表述含糊，后又被承认低估了即将到来的动荡期。
    'Fine' is left vague and later conceded to undersell a coming period of turbulence.

正文 Main text

上周，在包括 PHP2Shell 和一个长期存在的 GitLab 漏洞在内的大量严重漏洞披露之后，人们创造了“黑色十月”一词，标志着 AI 模型的网络风险开始成为现实。在我开始写这篇文章时，新闻周期似乎已稍微稳定下来，但随后又传来了 Binance 另一起重大加密安全事件的消息。加密行业有着非常悠久的被黑客攻击的历史，因此它并不是表明漏洞转化为新的社会危害的最佳行业。尽管如此，我们仍然在很大程度上正在经历这一转变所带来的影响。

Last week coined the term "Black October" after an onslaught of critical vulnerability disclosures, including PHP2Shell and a long-standing GitLab bug, signified the moment when the cyber risks of AI models started to materialize. It seemed like the news cycle had stabilized a bit when I started writing this, but then there was news about another major crypto security incident at Binance. The crypto industry has a very long history of hacks, so it's not the best industry to indicate vulnerabilities translating into new societal harm. Still, we are very much still living through the implications of this transition.

对网络安全领域正在发生的事情的最佳猜测是，幕后工作只是加速了，引入了更多人和资源来尝试修补互联网——《华尔街日报》报道称网络第一响应者不堪重负，这似乎表明系统正在发挥作用。我们正生活在漏洞指数级上升的这一情节中，而这一趋势需要很长时间才能在 S 型曲线的顶部趋于平缓。

The best guess for what is happening in the cybersecurity space is that the behind-the-scenes work has only accelerated, bringing more people and resources in to try and patch the internet — the Wall Street Journal reported that cyber first responders are strained, which seems like the system working. We're living through this plot of an exponential rise in vulnerabilities, which will take a long time to level off at the top of the sigmoid.

**四月份的开源权重 Mythos 本会没事？**

**Open-weight Mythos in April would've been fine?**

当 Claude Mythos 被宣布时，它被预告为一种新型网络武器，如果落入坏人之手，将导致大规模社会动荡。

When Claude Mythos was announced, it was previewed as if it was a new class of cyber weapon, where if it ended up in the wrong hands it would've caused mass societal destabilization.

看来这是错的。

It seems like this was wrong.

无论从哪个角度看，GLM-5.3 都是跨越能力门槛的模型，而且几乎没有公开证据表明情况发生了很大变化，此时距离模型权重发布已过去一个多月。

By all measures, GLM-5.3 is the model that crosses that threshold of capability, and there’s little public evidence that much has changed, and we’re over a month out of the model weight release.

事实上，我想更进一步说。如果 Claude Mythos 被意外以开放权重形式发布，世界似乎大体上会安然无恙。是的，网络安全事件会明显增加，模型泄露对社会不利，但这种情况看起来更像是加速，而非风险上的阶跃变化。

In fact, I would go further. If Claude Mythos was accidentally released as open-weight, it seems like the world would have been more or less fine. Yes, there would be a clear increase in cybersecurity incidents and it would be bad for society if the model leaked, but it would be a scenario that looks more like an acceleration than a step change in risk.

这波新的开放权重恐慌的主要鼓吹者实际上一直在做出一个可证伪的预测——即当前的开放权重模型将通过破坏我们的网络基础设施，造成重大的、前所未有的 AI 危害。

The leading proponents of this new wave of open-weight fear mongering have effectively been making a falsifiable prediction — that the current open-weight models are going to cause substantial, not seen before AI harms by crippling our cyber infrastructure.

根据我掌握的所有证据（我已在上文展开），他们注定是错的。至少现在，在争论开放权重风险这么多年后，我们将开始得到一些真正的答案。

Based on all the evidence I have, which I’ve unpacked above, they’re set up to be wrong. At least now, after so many years of debating open weight risks, we will start to get some real answers.

为了开始反思，我想做更多的尽职调查，并找到一些证据来证明 AI 安全社区的反应就好像开放权重的 Mythos 会是一场真正的灾难，而不仅仅是一个令人头痛的问题。这是我在经历网络讨论后得到的鲜明印象。我受到批评说，前沿 AI 社区从未说过这会是一场“真正的灾难”，但不管他们到底说了什么，关键始终在于人们如何听取和内化你的信息。

To start the reflection, I wanted to do more due diligence and track down some receipts on the claim that the AI safety community was reacting as if open-weight Mythos would be an actual disaster, and not just a painful headache. This was distinctly my impression having lived through the online discourse. I’ve gotten criticism that the frontier AI community never said it would be a “true disaster,” but regardless of exactly what they said, it is always about how people hear and internalize your information.

以下是一些我认为过于夸张的言论示例，这些言论来自知名人士，并渗透到公众、更重要的是 _联邦政府_ 的视角中，即认为这是一种网络武器，包括：

Here are some examples of the sort of language that I felt was too exaggerated and from prominent people, which spilled into the public and more importantly _federal government_ perspective that this was a cyber weapon include:

* Rob Wiblin，80,000 Hours 播客主持人，在其自己的播客中（2026 年 4 月 10 日）表示：“如果它落入犯罪分子或敌对国家网络行为者手中，那将是一场真正的灾难。”

* Rob Wiblin, host of the 80,000 Hours podcast, on his own podcast (April 10, 2026): “If it fell into the hands of criminals or hostile state cyber actors, it would be an actual disaster.”

* Anthropic 在 Project Glasswing 公告博客中（2026 年 4 月 7 日）写道：“鉴于 AI 的进展速度，此类能力不久就会扩散，可能超出那些致力于安全部署的行为者的控制。对经济、公共安全和国家安全造成的后果可能很严重。”

* Anthropic, in the Project Glasswing announcement blog (April 7, 2026): “Given the rate of AI progress, it will not be long before such capabilities proliferate, potentially beyond actors who are committed to deploying them safely. The fallout—for economies, public safety, and national security—could be severe.”

举一个实际上更合理的例子，以说明正确与夸张之间的细微界限，来自 Zvi Mowshowitz（我在很多问题上不同意他的观点）于 2026 年 4 月 9 日表示：“Claude Mythos 将给攻击者提供大量零日漏洞利用，几乎针对地球上所有软件……这将是一片混乱。”迄今为止，这种混乱仅限于那些运行大规模数字产品的人，这是好事，但情况可能会改变。

For one that is actually more reasonable, to illustrate the fine lines between correct and exaggerated, from Zvi Mowshowitz (who I don’t agree with a ton of things on) on April 9, 2026: “Claude Mythos would give attackers a cornucopia of zero-day exploits for essentially all the software on Earth … It would be chaos.” To date, this chaos has been confined to those running large-scale digital products, which is good, but could change.

我大体上仍然坚持几周前写的上述内容，但我对此感到紧张。我不是网络专家，我正在努力做出最有根据的预测。“我们所有的科技公司都需要加倍努力来保障我们的安全”和“公共基础设施实际上正在瘫痪”之间只有一线之隔。在撰写本文时，非常明显的是，在流行工具中发现了更多严重漏洞，并且修复程序正在构建中。

I largely still stand by my above writing a few weeks ago, but I’m nervous about it. I’m not a cyber expert, I’m working hard to make the most educated predictions I can. It’s a fine line between “all of our tech companies need to work extra hard to keep us secure” and “public infrastructure is actually going down.” It is very clear as of writing this that there are way more critical vulnerabilities which are being found in popular tools and fixes are being built.

例如，Epoch AI 最近发表了一篇不错的文章，介绍了他们内部使用 GLM-5.3 尝试寻找漏洞并将其串联成潜在攻击的工作。他们在 Firefox 中发现的实际漏洞利用是一个很好的例子，说明为什么网络风险需要多个因素同时具备才会造成危害：

For example, Epoch AI recently published a nice post on their internal work using GLM-5.3 to try and find vulnerabilities and chain them into what could be an attack. The actual exploit they found in Firefox is a good example of why cyber risks need multiple things to align for harm to be done:

我们使用 GLM 5.3 创建了一个网站，Firefox 用户只需访问该网站，无需进一步交互，攻击者就能从用户计算机中窃取文件。

We used GLM 5.3 to create a website that, simply when visited by a Firefox user and without further interaction, gave the attacker the ability to exfiltrate files from the user's computer.

在这里，漏洞利用存在于 Firefox 中，潜在的黑客需要植入一个寻找此漏洞的网站。这是可行的，并可能造成严重后果，但仅仅发现漏洞只是多阶段过程中的一步。

Here, the exploit is in Firefox, and a would-be hacker needs to plant a website that is looking for this exploit. This is doable, and could have serious consequences, but just finding the exploit is only one step in a multi-stage process.

在之前的文章中，我应该更具体地说明对于网络安全的未来而言“fine”意味着什么，在这种情况下，它意味着要更多地认可一些 AI 安全社区的观点，他们合理地将此讨论为网络不稳定的新时代。反思之后，我不确定“fine”是否是描述不可避免之事——被迫的行业更新，转向与软件协作的新方式——的合适词语，这显然代价高昂且伴随着艰辛的工作。如果你已经对 AI 带来的挑战感到忧虑，我认为“fine”实际上非常合适。如果你没有考虑到一段动荡时期，那么“fine”就低估了。我已经写过很多次，我属于前者，困难将会到来，群体——但我认为人类擅长应付问题并修补眼前的担忧。

In the previous post, I should've been more specific on what "fine" is for a future of cybersecurity, and in this case it means giving more credit to some of the AI safety community that discussed this reasonably as a new era in cyber instability. Reflecting on it, I'm not sure fine is the right word to describe what is inevitably coming – a forced industry update to a new way of working with software, which will clearly be expensive and accompanied by some hard work. If you're already somber about the challenges coming from AI, I think fine is actually very suitable. If you didn't price in a period of turbulence, fine undersells. I've written many times that I am in the former, difficulty will come, group — but I think humans are good at muddling through problems and patching immediate concerns.

我的观点更接近于：开放模型 _正在提供平衡_ 在攻防方程中，而 Mythos 更早出现也不会大规模颠覆这一点。我怀疑大多数这类公司会比攻击者更快地采用开放权重模型，因为大多数攻击者比这些公司资源更少。我们正在观察平衡的一个关键针是攻击者在美国或其盟友范围内造成物理伤害的意愿。目前，虽然我们的对手可能已经使用这些模型和漏洞以隐蔽方式大规模提取信息，但尚不清楚是否有方会通过将这些模型全力推向公共机构来造成大规模伤害。

What my view was closer to is that open models _are providing balance_ in the offense-defense equation, and that Mythos coming sooner wouldn't have massively upended this. I suspect most of these companies would be faster to adopt open-weight models than attackers, as most attackers are lower resourced than these companies. A key needle we're watching the balance on is how willing attackers are to cause physical harm within the U.S. or its allies. For now, while our adversaries have likely used these models and exploits to extract information en-masse in a clandestine way, it is not clear if there is a party who will cause mass harm by pushing the full force of these models at public institutions.

我觉得这条推文以一种有趣的方式概括了黑客活动中的人员/动机缺口：

I thought this Tweet was a funny way to summarize some of the personnel / motive gaps in hacking:

一旦你不再把威胁行为者视为想方设法闯入系统的黑客，而是开始把他们视为拥有明确业务流程的企业，生活就会轻松许多。

Life is a lot easier once you stop seeing threat actors as hackers who want to break into systems by any means possible, and start seeing them as businesses with well defined business processes.

面包店不会因为狗粮短缺就转行生产狗粮。ClickFix 和钓鱼行为者也不会仅仅因为出现了一个严重 CVE，就放下一切去用 vibecode 编写漏洞利用。

A bakery isn't going to start making dog food because there's a dog food shortage. ClickFix and Phishing actors aren't going to drop everything to vibecode exploits just because a critical CVE came out.

唯一在利用该漏洞的行为者，正是那些以利用漏洞为惯用手法（MO）的行为者……这并非巧合。如果你理解威胁行为者的行为模式，这完全是可以预测的。

The fact that the only actors exploiting this are the actors whose MO is exploiting things... isn't a coincidence. It's entirely predictable if you understand threat actor behavior.

在我看来，我预计这些漏洞大多是由封闭模型发现的，但我对这个预测的误差范围很大。很多事情取决于开放模型在短期内实际如何被使用。如今，开放模型的 serving 成本很高，而且用于 serving 最大模型的算力极度售罄（我怀疑如果开放模型很快被用于黑客攻击，那会是通过一个安全性较差的开放模型 API）。长期来看，我非常有信心，随着计算供应变得充足，开放模型的利润率和价格将会下降，但短期内，封闭模型肯定更具价格优势。我的许多立场都是为了确保我们能够达到那个未来，否则少数几家公司正在构建生产大量软件（带有漏洞）的工具，同时也在销售修复这些软件的工具。

Where I sit, I expect most of these vulnerabilities are being found by closed models, but I have very high error bars on this prediction. A lot hinges on how open models are actually being used in the near term. Today, open models are expensive to serve and the compute to serve the largest models is extremely sold out (I’d suspect that if open models are used in a hack soon, it’s through a poorly secured open model API). In the long-term, I am very confident that margins and prices on open models will be driven down as computing supply grows abundant, but in the near term the closed models are definitely more price effective. Many of my positions are making sure that we get to this future, as otherwise a few companies are building the tools that produce tons of software (with vulnerabilities) and also selling the tools to fix said software.

目前，我们正在处理积压的网络安全漏洞，同时从以人为中心的互联网过渡到由智能体中介的人类活动的封闭孤岛。

For now, we’re working through the backlog of cyber vulnerabilities as we transition from the human-focused internet to closed silos of human activity mediated by agents.

我认为所有这些更多地关乎 AI 的能力和扩散，而不是开放模型与封闭模型之争。事实上，我觉得在阅读更多以网络安全为导向的论述时（例如 Joshua Saxe 或 Chris Hughes 的一些例子），他们更关注生态系统视角，即利用率和网络整体损害率在 ChatGPT 之前很久就开始增长，以及这些近期事件如何符合趋势。只是趋势线是指数级的。下面是 Saxe 提供的一个趋势示例图——这类生态系统级别的图如果 AI 的能力和扩散是网络攻击的瓶颈，将会非常明显（它会看起来像一个阶跃变化）：

All of this I think is way more about AI’s capabilities and diffusion generally than anything about open vs. closed models. In fact, I feel like in reading more cybersecurity oriented discourse (some examples in Joshua Saxe or Chris Hughes), that they focus much more on the ecosystem perspective, how the rate of exploits and overall damages from cyber has been growing since long before ChatGPT, and how these recent events are on trend. It’s just that the trend line was exponential. An example plot of this trend is below from Saxe — these are the sort of ecosystem level plots which will be very clear if AI’s capability and diffusion is the bottleneck on cyber attacks (it would look like a step change):

在与接近 AI 社区的人，尤其是 AI 安全领域的人交谈时，我发现很多努力都花在弄清楚开放模型是否该受责备上，而我们对此的证据仍然极少。封闭模型在近期的黑客攻击中扮演了非常重要的角色，至少比开放模型更多。网络真的是一个社会问题，单靠 AI 公司无法解决。需要在更高层次的抽象上投入努力。这是我们需要用 AI 管理的众多风险和转型中的第一个，没有一个能仅靠以旧金山为中心的科技行业解决。

When talking to people who are close to the AI community, especially AI safety, I find a lot of effort going into figuring out if open models are to blame, which we still have extremely low evidence for. Closed models have played a very heavy part in the recent hacks, at least more than open models have. Cyber is really a societal problem, one which AI companies alone cannot solve. Effort needs to be spent at this higher level of abstraction. This is the first of many risks and transitions we’ll need to manage with AI, none of which can be solved solely by the San Francisco centric tech industry.
