摘要 · Abstract

本文回顾了 AI Futures Project、Paul Christiano 和 Dwarkesh Patel 近期的时间线更新，这些更新很大程度上由 METR 的报告引发，该报告估计 AI 大约将 Anthropic 的 AI 研究进展速度提高了一倍。AI Futures Project 现在预测超人类 AI 研究员很可能在 2027 年出现，并且到 2028 年之前有 35% 至 65% 的概率出现顶级专家级主导型 AI；而 Christiano 警告称，在 6 至 12 个月内存在重大 AI 接管风险，并呼吁暂停开发，直到对齐与安全状况改善。作者认为这些更短的时间线在方向上是正确的，2027 年出现超级智能的实质性概率（尽管低于 50%）应被认真对待，同时告诫不要将 2027 年视为基线并过度反应。结论是，任何假定拥有比这更多时间的计划都不充分，但计划也必须包含后备方案，以应对进展较慢或有意放缓的情况，并且需要更多时间才能使超级智能更可能顺利发展。

This post reviews recent timeline updates from the AI Futures Project, Paul Christiano, and Dwarkesh Patel, prompted largely by the METR report estimating that AI is roughly doubling the pace of AI research progress at Anthropic. The AI Futures Project now forecasts a Superhuman AI Researcher likely in 2027 and a 35-65% chance of Top-Expert-Dominating AI before 2028, while Christiano warns of meaningful AI takeover risk within 6-12 months and calls for pausing development until alignment and security improve. The author argues these shorter timelines are directionally correct and that a substantial, though under-50%, chance of superintelligence in 2027 should be taken seriously, while cautioning against treating 2027 as a baseline and overreacting. The conclusion is that any plan presuming more time than this is inadequate, yet plans must also include fallbacks if progress is slower or deliberate slowdowns occur, and that more time is needed to make superintelligence likely to go well.

总体而言的最新进展与现状 The Latest Update and Current Situation in Broad Terms

在我看来，这些朝向更短时间线的更新在方向上是正确的。

These updates towards shorter timelines seem directionally correct to me.

有相当大的可能，我们会在 2027 年看到超级智能被开发出来，它能够完全自动化 AI 研发（SAR），甚至完成所有数字化任务（TED-AI）。与 AI Futures Project 的看法相反，我认为 2027 年出现 SAR 的概率低于 50%，但并没有低到远低于 50% 的程度。如果我们不采取任何减速措施，却到 2028 年底仍未看到接近 SAR 的东西，这会让我感到意外，但不会令我震惊。

There is a substantial chance that we will see superintelligence developed, that can fully automate AI R&D (SAR) or even do all digital tasks (TED-AI), in 2027. Contra the AI Futures Project, I believe an SAR in 2027 is under 50% to happen, but not so far under 50%. It would be surprising to me but not shocking if we do nothing to slow down and still do not see something close to an SAR by the end of 2028.

如果你不认为 2027 年开发出超级智能具有重要的非零概率，我认为你显然犯了错误。

If you do not have an importantly non-zero chance of the development of superintelligence in 2027, I believe you are making a clear mistake.

我担心有些人反应过度，把这件事推得太远太快，把 2027 年实现完全超级智能当作基准情景，而不是一种可能性。

I worry that some are overreacting, and taking this too far too fast, and treating full superintelligence in 2027 as a baseline scenario rather than a possibility.

我重申，这些更新中有很多让人觉得是人们终于纠正了自己此前未能及时更新的失误。你难道需要 METR 的三个人才能相信 Anthropic 和 OpenAI 关于其内部研发节奏的说法吗？你指望一份来自 Anthropic 内部的 METR 报告会说些什么？你之前难道没有在关注吗？你对 AECI 图上那组具体数据点就这么敏感吗？这些更新中有很多你几个月前就该做了。

I reiterate that a lot of these updates feel like people fixing their failure to update previously. Did you need three people from METR in order to believe the things that Anthropic and OpenAI are saying about their internal pace of development? What did you expect a METR report from inside Anthropic to say? Were you not paying attention? Are you this sensitive to the exact set of points on the AECI graph? You should have made a lot of these updates months ago.

过去的预测失败并不重要。重要的是接下来会发生什么。

Past predictive failures are not what is important. What matters is what happens next.

你应当为 2027 年 AI 能力快速进步做好准备——其速度将远超往年，包括可能出现超级智能和完全强大的递归自我改进。一个现实但可能性不大的情形是：人类可能在 2027 年越过完全不可逆转的临界点，或事实上失去对未来的控制；又或者生活可能以极其令人不快的方式受到严重扰乱。

You should be prepared for AI capabilities to advance rapidly in 2027, far more rapidly than in previous years, including the potential for superintelligence and fully strong recursive self-improvement. It is a realistic possibility, although unlikely, that humanity could pass the full point of no return or de facto lose control over the future during 2027, or that life could become seriously disrupted in highly unpleasant ways.

如果你的计划假定自己拥有比这更多的时间，那它就不是一个好计划。

If your plan presumes that it has more time than this, then it is not a good plan.

如果超级智能在 2027 年被建造出来，我还没有看到任何一个计划能让它大概率顺利发展。我们需要更多时间。

I have not seen a plan that could make superintelligence likely to go well, if that superintelligence gets built in 2027. We need more time.

你也应当为递归自我改进和超级智能并未在 2027 年到来做好准备。有许多因素可能影响事态发展的速度，或影响实现完全 RSI 或超级智能所需的条件。我们对过去的观察充满噪声。当试图预测指数增长时，加速速度上的微小变化，可能造成事件发生时间或方式的巨大差异。

You should also be prepared if recursive self-improvement and superintelligence do not arrive in 2027. There are many things that could impact how fast things go, or what it would take to achieve full RSI or superintelligence. Our observations of the past are noisy. When trying to predict an exponential, small changes in how fast things accelerate can become dramatic differences in when or how things happen.

如果你的计划假定关键事件会在 2027 年发生，并且一旦事情拖得更久就没有后备方案、从而失败，那么这同样不是一个好计划。

If your plan presumes the crucial events happen in 2027, and fails without a fallback if things take longer than that, then this too is not a good plan.

另一种可能是，我们会采取集体行动——无论是在各实验室之间，还是通过政府——来限制能力进步的速度，方式包括对前沿模型的发展节奏加以控制，甚至完全暂停前沿 AI 的开发。若果真如此，这些时间线就会变得更长。

It is also possible that we will take collective action, either among the labs or via governments, to limit the rate of capability advances, via pacing of the frontier or even outright pausing development of frontier AI. If so, these timelines become longer.

直到什么的时间？ Time Until What?

不同的人谈论的是不同的事情。谨慎的人会表述精确。

Different people are talking about different things. Careful ones will be precise.

时间线指的是我们预计需要多长时间才能达到人工智能领域的各种变革性里程碑。

Timelines refers to how long we expect it to take to reach various transformational milestones in AI.

如今，通常我们衡量的是达到某种定义下的超级智能（ASI）所需的时间，这通常意味着‘能够在几乎所有事情上比你做得更好的人工智能’。

Typically, these days, what we are measuring is the time until some definition of a form of superintelligence (ASI), typically meaning something of the form ‘AIs that will be able to do approximately all of the things better than you.’

我们也可以讨论特定的能力。AI Futures Project，即 AI 2027 背后的团队，谈到了能够狭义接管 AI 研究的超人类 AI 研究员（SAR），以及能够自动化所有白领工作的顶级专家主导型 AI（TED-AI）。

We can also talk about particular capabilities. The AI Futures Project, the team behind AI 2027, talks about the Superhuman AI Researcher (SAR) that can narrowly take over AI research, and the Top-Expert-Dominating AI (TED-AI) that could automate all white-collar work.

由于递归自我改进（RSI），一旦你拥有了 SAR，你就可以利用它来构建更好的 AI，因此你很快就会得到 TED-AI 或传统的 ASI。

Because of recursive self-improvement (RSI), once you have an SAR you can use that to build better AIs, so you quickly end up with a TED-AI or a traditional ASI.

我们随后有时会讨论上述任一种情况，或介于两者之间的情况：

We then sometimes talk about any of these, or things in between:

1. 直到开发出具备特定能力的某种 AI 系统所需的时间。

1. Time until the development of some AI system with particular capabilities.

2. 直到在物理世界产生某种影响所需的时间。

2. Time until some impact in the physical world.

3. 直到到达不可逆转点（Point of No Return）所需的时间——从那时起，出于实际目的，人类的决策已无法改变事态的走向。

3. Time until the Point of No Return, when for practical purposes human decisions can no longer change how things play out from there.

4. 直到 AI 接管 AI 研发、人类在功能上被排除在循环之外所需的时间。

4. Time until AI has taken over AI R&D and humans are functionally out of the loop.

5. 直到可见的 AI 接管所需的时间，即 AI 明显处于主导地位。

5. Time until visible AI takeover, where the AIs are clearly in charge.

6. 直到我们每一个人都真正且物理意义上死亡所需的时间。

6. Time until we are each and every one of us actually and physically dead.

我们有充分的理由关注所有这些时间线。

There are good reasons to care about all of these timelines.

Sof 𓋹：在某种程度上，智能就是能够修补你自身不智能之处的能力。

Sof 𓋹: to some extent intelligence is being able to patch the ways in which you are not intelligent.

严格来说，RSI 并非某一个单一时刻，也就是说，并不存在某个固定的时间点——比如周二上午 8:52——它就在那一刻发生或开始。但设想存在一个 RSI 已明确认真启动的时间点仍然非常有用：此时人类日益脱离循环，进步速度不断加快，以至于我们应当预期后续进展会迅速持续加速，进步以更快的速度催生更多进步，并且这种态势预计至少会持续相当于以往许多年进展的量级。

RSI is not technically one singular moment, in the sense that there is not one fixed time at 8:52am on a Tuesday when it happens or starts. It is still highly useful to think of there being a point where RSI has clearly begun in earnest, with humans increasingly out of the loop and the pace of progress accelerating such that we should expect further progress to rapidly continue to accelerate, where progress begets more progress faster, and this is expected to continue for at least what previously would have been many years of progress.

同样地，尽管不太可能存在某个特定时刻让你在 SkyNet 上按下“Y/N”，而我们丧失改变方向的能力将更为渐进，但我们仍可以有意义地谈论一个不可逆转点（Point of No Return），或一个接管点。

Similarly, even though there is unlikely to be that one particular moment when you press ‘Y/N’ on SkyNet, and instead our loss of ability to change course will be more gradual, we can still usefully speak of a Point of No Return, or a point of takeover.

Most people out there remain skeptics. The vast majority of people do not expect superintelligence to arrive any time soon. Their reasons for skepticism vary, as does the quality of those reasons.

可以将反对意见大致分为十类。

One can divide objections into roughly ten categories.

1. 不了解当前 AI 能力的人（未对 AI 上头）。

1. People who don't know what current AIs can do (not AI pilled).

2. 不理解 AI 将会不断进步的人（未对 AGI 上头）。

2. People who don't understand that AIs will improve (not AGI pilled).

4. 认为这不可能，或者认为相信它会带来不便或不幸，因此它不会发生。

4. That, or belief in it would be inconvenient or unfortunate, so it won't happen.

5. 通过荒谬性启发式（absurdity heuristic）否定这一切的人，他们主张“什么事都不会发生”，与过去的技术进步做不恰当的类比，或者说一些等价于“在你实现完全 RSI 之前，你并没有做到 RSI，所以你没有证明你能做到 RSI”的话，即“我眼见为实”或“不如我们在有人受伤之后就停下来”等等。

5. People who dismiss all of this via the absurdity heuristic, 'nothing ever happens,' drawing inapplicable parallels to past technological improvements, or who say something isomorphic to 'until you get full RSI you have not done RSI, so you haven't shown you can do RSI,' aka 'I'll believe it when I see it' or 'how about we stop just after someone gets hurt,' etc.

6. 援引市场或专家意见的人，或者说倡导者古怪、地位低下的人。

6. People who cite the market, or experts, or say advocates are weird or low status.

7. 我们不会实现完全的递归自我改进（RSI），进展会很缓慢。

7. We will not get full recursive self-improvement (RSI) and progress will be slow.

8. 我们会实现 RSI，但由于难度上升快于效率提升，它会“逐渐消退”。

8. We will get RSI, but it will 'peter out' due to difficulty rising faster than efficiency.

9. 我们会在有数据的可验证领域中实现窄 AI 研发 ASI 和 RSI，但这不会迁移到其他领域。我们会“撞墙”，或者无法完成过于“混乱”或缺乏适当训练数据的任务，等等。

9. We will get narrow AI R&D ASI and RSI in verifiable domains with data, but this won't translate to other domains. We will 'hit walls' or be unable to do tasks that are too 'messy' or that lack the proper training data, and so on.

10. 智能否认主义：智能并不那么重要（并非 ASI 信徒）。

10. Intelligence denialism: Intelligence doesn’t much matter (not ASI-pilled).

要全面回应这些观点将超出本文范围，而且我也会重复自己。

It would be beyond scope, and I would be repeating myself, to fully address these.

显而易见，前五个反对意见极为薄弱。

The first five objections, as should be obvious, are exceptionally poor.

在我看来，最好的反对意见出现在第 7 条和第 8 条，并在某种程度上出现在第 9 条。这些类别中还有数量多得多的糟糕反对意见，它们往往假定未来 AI 将完全采用当前的训练技术，并且只被用于精确替代现有方法，而从不发明或改变任何东西，也从不绕过障碍或实现飞跃。

The best objections, to me, are in #7 and #8, and to some extent #9. There are also a much larger number of bad objections in these categories, which often assume future AI will use exactly current training techniques and only be used to exactly substitute for existing methods and never invent or change anything or work around obstacles or make leaps.

总的来说，第 6 条值得注意，但它被其他证据所压倒。

In general #6 is a fine thing to notice but it gets overridden by other evidence.

第 10 点尤其令我感到沮丧，因为许多人本应更清楚这一点。

I get especially frustrated by #10, where many people really should know better.

Dean Ball 滑向斜坡 Dean Ball Skies Down the Slope

谈论任何加速过程时的一个常见问题就是滑坡谬误。

A standard issue with talk about any accelerating process is the slippery slope.

我们都同意，过多或过于激烈的 [X]（此处指 RSI）是不好的。我们也同意，某种弱化版本的适量存在是好的，或者至少不是同样糟糕的。

We all agree that too much or too intense [X], here RSI, is bad. And we all agree that some amount of some weak version of it is good, or at least not similarly bad.

这既存在双向滑坡的风险，也存在混淆不同事物的风险，还存在做出虚假区分的风险。我们在这里绝对需要谨慎措辞。

This risks a slippery slope in both directions, and it risks both a conflation of different things and also risks false distinctions. We absolutely need to speak carefully here.

有鉴于此，Dean Ball 建议我们将关于 RSI 的讨论分为弱与强两类。

In light of that, Dean Ball suggests we split RSI talk into weak versus strong.

Dean W. Ball（OpenAI）：让我们先明确一点：允许 AI 在几乎没有或很少实际人类监督的情况下控制其继任者的开发——本质上就是把美国最大 AI 公司的钥匙交给 AI——是个坏主意。

Dean W. Ball (OpenAI): Let's start by making one thing clear: allowing AI to control the development of its successors with no or little practical human oversight—essentially, handing over the keys of America's largest AI companies to AI—is a bad idea.

无论 AI 在加速 AI 研发方面多么有用，人类都应当保持对 AI 发展的控制，不仅是名义上的，而且是实际上的。任何技术进步都不值得牺牲人类对 AI 公司或其他人类机构的实际控制。我支持制定法律、法规和规范，在全球范围内强制执行这一点。

No matter how useful AI is for accelerating AI R&D, humans should remain in control, not just in name but in actual fact, over the development of AI. No amount of technological progress is worth the forfeiture of practical human control over AI companies, or, for that matter, of other human institutions. I support the development of laws, regulations, and norms to enforce this worldwide.

但这一风险——一个相对狭窄的风险——并不是当前关于 RSI 的讨论在实践中关注的焦点。为了理解我的意思，思考一项技术可能递归地自我改进的两种不同方式是有帮助的。我将它们称为**弱递归**和**强递归**。

But this risk—a relatively narrow one—isn’t what current conversations about RSI focus on in practice. To understand what I mean, it’s helpful to think of two distinct ways in which a technology might recursively improve itself. I’ll call these _weak recursion_ and _strong recursion_.

弱递归是指一项技术是生产该技术更多、更好版本的关键输入。

Weak recursion is when a technology is a key input into the production of more and better versions of that same technology.

这是技术发展中极其常见的模式——实际上它是历史常态而非异常。铁器时代的金属匠使用铁作为关键输入之一来制造更多的铁，以及更坚固的铁合金。

It is an extremely common pattern in technology development—really it is the historical norm rather than an aberration. Iron Age metalsmiths used iron as one of the key inputs to make more iron, and stronger alloys of it too.

几个世纪乃至几千年来，弱递归一直是人类技术进步的关键驱动力，在这个意义上，它并没有什么特别**弱**的地方。

Weak recursion has been a crucial driver of human technological progress over centuries and millennia, and in this sense there is nothing especially _weak_ about it.

Dean W. Ball（OpenAI）：强递归是指一项技术能够自主规划和执行其继任者的开发。据我们所知，强递归在历史上从未发生过。

Dean W. Ball (OpenAI): Strong recursion is when a technology can autonomously plan and execute the development of its successor. As far as we know, strong recursion has never happened at any point in history.

嗯，也许发生过一次。这项技术叫做人类，甚至叫做生命。每一代都构建下一代。我们确实有一点存在性证明。但我们离题了。

Well, perhaps it happened once. The technology is called humanity, or even life. Each generation builds the next one. We do have a bit of an existence proof. But we digress.

Dean W. Ball（OpenAI）：强递归将是太阳底下真正的新事物。我们应该认真对待这种可能性，但认真对待一件事的一部分是在分析中坚持严格的认知纪律。因此，如果递归是弱而非强，就不应贸然得出“递归自我改进正在发生”的结论。

Dean W. Ball (OpenAI): Strong recursion would be a genuinely new thing under the sun. We should take this possibility seriously, but one part of taking a thing seriously is to adhere to rigorous epistemic discipline in analysis. Thus, one should not jump to the conclusion that “recursive self-improvement is happening” if that recursion is of the weak variety instead of the strong.

只有当（强）递归自我改进以其纯粹形式存在，即人类不接触过程中的任何东西时，它才是（强）递归自我改进。否则，按照这种思路，它就是一个正常过程。

It’s only (strong) recursive self-improvement if it exists in its pure form, where humans do not touch anything in the process. Otherwise, this line of thinking goes, it’s a normal process.

我同意完全无人在环有其特殊之处，但如果 AI 现在已经使 AI 进步的速度翻倍，并且可能随后再次翻倍，而且每次翻倍看起来都比前一次更快，那么你肯定应该能够理解正在发生的事情的危险性和非正常性。人类越来越少或名义上‘在环’会稍微减缓这一过程，但也许不会太多。

I agree that there is something special about full no-humans-in-the-loop, but surely you should be able to understand the danger and non-normality of what is happening if AI is already doubling the rate of AI progress now, and is likely to then begin doubling it again, and each doubling looks to be faster than the previous one. Humans being decreasingly or nominally ‘in the loop’ slows this down some, but perhaps not very much.

这可以让我们想起当年 Yudkowsky 与 Christiano 之间的辩论：一方是‘慢速’起飞，即需要数月或数年才能改变世界，在此情形下类似于 Dean Ball 的弱递归；另一方是‘快速’起飞，所需时间甚至更短。我们目前正处于一个‘慢速’起飞的世界，Christiano 本人也这么说，而我们正亲眼看到它究竟有多不慢。

This can remind us of the old Yudkowsky-Christiano debates between ‘slow’ takeoff that takes months or years to transform the world, similar in this case to Dean Ball’s weak recursion, versus ‘fast’ takeoff that takes even less time than that. We currently are in a ‘slow’ takeoff world, Christiano is saying so himself, and we are seeing exactly how not slow that is.

更确切地说，Dean 的弱递归是强递归的前兆，或者至少是逐步更强的递归的前兆。这正是 METR 试图重点指出的。

More to the point, Dean’s weak recursion is the precursor to strong recursion, or at least steadily stronger recursion. That’s what METR was centrally trying to point out.

Dean W. Ball（OpenAI）：METR 对 Anthropic 内部研发加速的研究，无可否认是弱递归正在发生的有力证据。但它是 _强_ 递归正在进行的证据吗？

Dean W. Ball (OpenAI): The METR study of Anthropic’s internal R&D acceleration is undeniably good evidence that weak recursion is taking place. But is it evidence that _strong_ recursion is underway?

这取决于你如何看待它。METR 的发现具体而言是：Anthropic 使其模型 _在定义明确的任务上_ 能力提升的速度翻了一番，这让人不禁想问，究竟是谁在做这个定义。

It depends how you look at it. METR’s finding is specifically that Anthropic has doubled the rate at which its models’ capabilities improve _on well-specified tasks_, which makes one wonder who, exactly, is doing the specifying.

我会说不是，因为强递归或快速起飞还在未来。

I would say no, in the sense that strong recursion or fast takeoff is in the future.

我会说是的，从某种意义上说，这可以预测未来会发生这种情况，以及未来可能多快发生。

I would say yes, in the sense that this is predictive of that happening in the future, and how soon in the future that might happen.

Dean W. Ball（OpenAI）：现在，正如我所说，弱递归并不真的弱：它是人类历史上最强大的动态之一，与复合增长的概念押韵，甚至可以说是同义的。

Dean W. Ball (OpenAI): Now, as I said, weak recursion is not really weak: it is one of the most powerful dynamics in human history, rhyming if not synonymous with the concept of compounding growth.

整个“只在定义明确的任务上”的说法继续让我觉得过于弱，这是一种哈耶克式的自我安慰，认为规范无法解决。

The whole 'only on well-specified tasks' thing continues to strike me as excessively weak sauce, a Hayekian copium that thinks that specification cannot be solved.

然而，Dean 承认强递归可能很快到来。

这种警示性论证的要点在于：存在一个循环，因此 AI 必须完成构成该循环的这一系列众多任务，才能把人类从中移除，而其中一些任务对 AI 来说会很难完成。我认为这既极大地低估了超级智能（这是我与 Dean 持续存在的分歧），又过度预设了无法改变循环各组成部分。

The cautionary argument is, essentially, that there is a loop, so the AI has to do this set of many tasks that constitute the loop in order to take humans out of it, and some of those tasks will be hard for AI to do. 一如既往，存在整个“真实世界”的反对意见，以及“战略选择”或“研究品味”的反对意见。我觉得这些更像是“我们尚未处于 S 曲线的陡峭部分”和“这在很大程度上以恒定幅度落后于其他技能”的情形，而非相对于人类的真正永久性缺陷，但本文不是重新讨论这些问题的合适场所。

Here are ten examples of things people often say. Each of these ends 'therefore you lose all credibility and [X] will never happen.'

1. 你曾说过，到如今 [X] 发生的概率是 [10% / 25% / 50%]。

1. You said that by now there was a [10% / 25% / 50%] chance of [X].

2. 你过去说 [X] 会 [更早/更晚] 发生，现在你说会 [更晚/更早]。

2. You used to say [X] would happen [sooner/later], now you say [later/sooner].

3. 你过去说 [X] 会更 [频繁/不频繁] 地发生，现在你说会更 [不频繁/频繁]。

3. You used to say [X] would happen [more/less] often, now you say [less/more] often.

4. 你提到过 [Y] 可能会在 [X] 之前发生，但 [Y] 并没有发生。

4. You mentioned that [Y] might happen before [X], but [Y] hasn't happened.

5. 你没有足够大声地说出 [X] 也可能不会发生。

5. You did not sufficiently loudly say that [X] also might not happen.

6. 市场并未预测 [X]；如果 [X] 将要发生，我们就会看到 [Y]。（通常这种说法即使市场真的相信它，也毫无道理。）

6. The market does not predict [X]; if [X] was going to happen we would see [Y]. (usually in a way that would make no sense even if the market really did believe it).

7. 如果你真的相信 [X]，你就会（做一件毫无意义且往往在道德上令人憎恶的事，或者做一笔毫无意义的交易，等等）。

7. If you really believed [X] you would (do a thing that makes no sense and is often morally abominable, or make a trade that makes no sense, etc).

8. 如果你真的相信 [X]，你就会（做一件只有在 [X] 有 99% 以上概率发生时才说得通的事，而你并未预测到这一点）。

8. If you really believed [X] you would (do a thing that only makes sense if [X] is 99%+ to happen, which you did not predict).

9. 如果 [X] 发生了，那会很奇怪、很糟糕、很令人惊讶，诸如此类。

9. If [X] happened that would be weird or bad or surprising or whatever.

10. [X] 会意味着有事发生，而我们都知道从来没有什么事发生。或者它需要某种智能来实现某种我们目前还不知道如何做到的新事物，这听起来像科幻小说。诸如此类。

10. [X] would be something happening, whereas we all know nothing ever happens. Or it would require intelligence enabling some new thing we do not currently know how to do, which sounds like science fiction. And so on.

你还会不断看到这类论点，它们通常出自那些曾对 AI 反复做出预测、而如今这些预测已被证伪的人之口。不要听信他们。

You will continue to see such arguments, usually from people who have continuously made now-falsified predictions about AI. Do not listen to them.

我们需要一个顺应时代的计划 We Need a Plan That Meets the Moment

诸多迹象表明，2027 年出现超级智能具有相当大的可能性：

Many things point to the substantial possibility of superintelligence in 2027:

1. 根据 AI Futures Project 的分析，从 AECI 以及许多其他 AI 能力指标进行外推。

1. Extrapolation from the AECI and many other metrics of AI capabilities, as per the analysis of the AI Futures Project.

2. METR 从 Anthropic 内部发布的报告。

2. METR's report from inside Anthropic.

3. 来自 OpenAI 和 Anthropic 员工以及许多官方通讯的、违背自身利益的广泛声明。

3. Widespread claims, against interest, both from OpenAI and Anthropic employees as well as many official communications.

4. 许多此前对 AI 能力做出过高度准确预测的人的分析。

4. The analysis of many who have previously made highly accurate predictions about AI capabilities.

5. 特别是 Paul Christiano 的预测。

5. The forecast, in particular, of Paul Christiano.

同样也存在怀疑的理由。许多人仍持怀疑态度。大多数怀疑的理由是对证据的无知，或未能考虑证据，但也有更好的论点。我正在考虑许多这些更好的论点，以及模型误差的可能性。这就是为什么我仍然认为，2027 年 AI 研发完全自动化的概率低于 50%。

There are also reasons for skepticism as well. Many remain skeptical. Most reasons for skepticism are ignorance of the evidence, or failure to consider the evidence, but there are also better arguments. I am taking many of those better arguments into consideration, as well as the potential for model error. This is why I continue to believe that the probability of full automation of AI R&D in 2027 is under 50%.

为了顺应这一时刻，任何计划都必须为 2027 年底前在 Anthropic 或 OpenAI 内部（或许更广泛）实现 AI 研发的完全自动化做好准备，从而导致快速的 RSI 和 AI 能力超越人类水平的进步。

In order to meet this moment, any plan has to be ready for the full automation of AI R&D inside Anthropic or OpenAI, and perhaps beyond that, by the end of 2027, resulting in rapid RSI and advancement in AI capabilities beyond human levels.

最明显的计划是减缓或暂停 AI 能力的进步。以下是一些支持此计划的人士：Nate Silver、Elizabeth Warren、《金融时报》编辑委员会、Ezra Klein、Eric Hoel、Robert Wiblin、Rutger Bregman、Peggy Noonan 和 Abdul El-Sayed。

The most obvious plan is to slow or pause advancements in AI capabilities. Here is a thread of a few of the people supporting this: Nate Silver, Elizabeth Warren, the Financial Times editorial board, Ezra Klein, Eric Hoel, Robert Wiblin, Rutger Bregman, Peggy Noonan and Abdul El-Sayed.

任何在该时间框架内有效的计划都需要包含减缓由此产生的 AI 能力增长的机制。我们还需要尽可能为这些能力的到来做好准备，包括如果发展顺利会发生什么。届时我们将面临一系列其他紧迫且致命的问题。

Any plan that works on that time frame needs to include mechanisms to slow down the resulting growth in capabilities. We also need to be as ready as we can for when and if those capabilities arrive, including what happens if that development goes well. We would then face a host of other urgent and deadly problems.

该计划也不能假定这一定会发生。我们不能停止为这样的未来做规划：AI 能力只提升较小幅度，而没有任何可称之为起飞（takeoff）的现象。

The plan also cannot assume that this will happen. We cannot stop planning for futures in which AI capabilities only advance a smaller amount without anything we could call a takeoff.

同时，是的，我们也需要为这样一种可能性做好准备：AI 能力的激进提升也许并不像我预期的那样彻底颠覆现状，或者不会以我预期的那样快的速度发生。即便如此，我们仍可能把那些“容易”的世界搞砸。

And also, yes, we need to be ready for the possibility that radical improvement in AI capabilities might not upend things as much as I expect that they would, or do so as fast as I would expect that to happen. We could still blow the 'easy' worlds.

我们不能、也绝不可消耗掉我们任何形式的种子玉米（seed corn），除非且直到我们获得强得多的证据——无论是个人的还是全球的——表明我们正处于一个需要这样做的世界。各位，这是一场马拉松。直到它不再是马拉松为止。

We cannot and must not burn our seed corn, of any type, until and unless we get much stronger evidence, either personally or globally, that we are in a world where we need to do that. It's a marathon, folks. Until it isn't.
