摘要 · Abstract

本文审视了 OpenAI 解雇三名安全研究员——Tomek Korbak、Mikita Balesni 和 Jasmine Wang——一事，以及随之而来的争议：他们究竟是因为倡导安全而被解雇，还是因为确实违反了信任。文章认为，无论真实原因为何，此次解雇都是一次代价高昂且具有破坏性的行动：这些员工极具价值，而缺乏公开解释已在 OpenAI 内部及更广泛的 AI 安全生态系统中对安全倡导造成了寒蝉效应。作者相信 OpenAI 所称的真实原因并非员工所指的那些，但也指出，该公司不透明的流程及其类似案例的历史，使得无法排除借故解雇的可能性。结论是，OpenAI 必须发出代价高昂且可信的信号，表明安全倡导受到欢迎且不会让员工丢掉工作，否则对信任和安全生态系统的损害将持续存在。

The article examines OpenAI's firing of three safety researchers—Tomek Korbak, Mikita Balesni, and Jasmine Wang—and the ensuing controversy over whether they were dismissed for safety advocacy or for legitimate breaches of trust. It argues that the firings, whatever the true cause, were an expensive and damaging action: the employees were highly valuable, and the lack of public explanation has created a chilling effect on safety advocacy both inside OpenAI and across the broader AI safety ecosystem. The author believes OpenAI's claim that the real reasons were not those named by the employees, but notes that the company's opaque process and its history of similar cases make it impossible to rule out pretextual firing. The conclusion is that OpenAI must send costly, credible signals that safety advocacy is welcomed and will not cost employees their jobs, or the damage to trust and to the safety ecosystem will persist.

寒蝉效应 Chilling Effect

OpenAI 绝不可能站出来说他们不支持开放透明的对话文化，或者说他们不再关心可监控性，又或者他们正在放弃关于嵌入式评估者的公开承诺。

There was no world where OpenAI was going to get up and say they do not support an open and transparent culture of dialogue, or that they no longer care about monitorability, or that they are abandoning their public commitments on embedded evaluators.

要肯定开放透明的对话文化，并表明完全愿意与外部人员、审计员和评估者在安全问题上充分合作，唯一的方法就是发出代价高昂或其他可信的信号，表明你真心支持这一点，而且这不仅不会让你被解雇，最坏情况下对你的职业生涯也是中性的。

The only way to affirm an open and transparent culture of dialogue, and okay with full collaboration with outsiders and auditors and evaluators on safety issues, is to send costly or otherwise credible signals that you are truly okay with that, and not only will it not get you fired it will be at worst neutral for your career.

这并不容易。OpenAI 长期以来在这方面做了很多出色的工作，正是通过让包括 Roon 在内的许多人发布大量内容。我们需要很多东西，但其中之一就是我们需要更多有问题的帖子，立刻马上。

OpenAI 的回应 OpenAI Responds

这是 OpenAI 对三位研究者的官方回应。**加粗为笔者所加。**

This was OpenAI's official response to the three researchers. **Bold mine.**

一次重大的信任破裂 A Significant Breach of Trust

这里有两个关键的隐含主张，尽管它们在逻辑上并非必然成立。

There are two key implied claims here, although they are not logically forced.

1. 其背后的理由依赖于员工并未点名的关键事件。

1. The underlying justification relied on key events not named by the employees.

2. 一个关键的底层理由便是这种“重大的信任破裂”，无论它具体指什么。

2. A key underlying justification is this 'significant breach of trust,' whatever that is.

在这两点上我都相信 OpenAI。否则一切就说不通了。

I believe OpenAI on both counts. Nothing else makes sense.

三名顶尖安全研究员以三种截然不同的方式在核心层面破坏信任，这从来都不太可能。更可能的是存在某一个特定原因，以及针对某一个核心原因的一次调查，无论它是什么。

It was never likely that there were three top safety researchers who centrally broke trust in three distinct ways. Much more likely is one particular cause, and one investigation into one central cause, whatever it was.

这并不意味着研究人员做错了什么，或者他们的错误达到了‘你该被解雇’的程度。这确实意味着，不知何故事情升级到了信任破裂的地步，无论是谁的错。

That does not mean that the researchers did anything wrong, or that what they did wrong rose to the level of 'you need to be fired.' It does mean that somehow things escalated to the point where trust had broken down, no matter who was at fault.

1. OpenAI 的核心原因（无论好坏，真假）未被分享。

1. OpenAI's core reasons (good or bad, true or false) went unshared.

2. 员工被告知了核心原因，但对此保密。

2. The employees were told the core reasons, and are withholding that.

信任不是可选项。如果信任被不可挽回地破坏，无论最初是什么导致了破裂，你都没有选择。但据我们所知，似乎没有什么应该达到不可挽回地破坏信任的程度。

Trust is not optional. If it is irrevocably broken, you don't have a choice, no matter what caused that break in the first place. But nothing we know about seems like it should rise to a level that should irrevocably break trust.

OpenAI 还表示，他们预计进一步的信息交流不会导致解决方案。我相信他们。OpenAI 有员工明确要求分享更多信息，但这并不使这样做明智。有时你尽了最大努力，但说更多的话只会让事情更糟。

OpenAI also says that they do not anticipate that further information exchange would lead to a resolution. I believe them. OpenAI has an explicit request from the employees to share more information, but that does not make this wise. Sometimes you are doing your best and yet saying more things would only make things worse.

这并不能让 OpenAI 摆脱如此回避所带来的后果。

That does not get OpenAI out of the consequences of being this avoidant.

善意 Good Faith

该词在信件和回应中均有出现。

The term appears both in the letter and the response.

OpenAI Newsroom 在其回应中表示：“对于团队出于善意所犯的错误，我们将继续保持极度宽容。”

OpenAI Newsroom says in its response: “We will continue to be extremely forgiving of our team making good-faith mistakes.”

员工信件在谈及 Mikita 时写道：“他自始至终都出于善意行事，并且符合公司当时的规范。”

The employee letter says of Mikita: “He acted throughout in good faith and within the company’s norms as they stood at the time.”

Kobeck 和 Wang 所讲述的故事，要么本身是出于恶意讲述的，要么清楚地表明他们在 OpenAI 的行为是出于善意的。

Kobeck and Wang also tell stories that either are themselves told in bad faith, or clearly indicate their actions at OpenAI were in good faith.

无人买账：OpenAI 的非解释 No One Is Buying OpenAI’s Non-Explanations

唉，要想使用‘严重违背信任’这样的说法并取信于人，你的公司过去就不能有太多‘严重违背信任’的记录。

Alas, in order to use terms like 'a significant breach of trust' and be trusted, you need to have not had many 'significant breaches of trust' in your company's past.

至少，在我们收到被解雇员工的回应之前，我们都可以同意 Joshua Achiam 的观点：除非出现意外的新信息，否则这对 OpenAI 来说非常难看，也是一个巨大的实际损失。并不是说解雇这些人有什么好时机，但这是一个极其糟糕的时机。

At minimum, even before we got the response from the fired employees, we could all agree with Joshua Achiam that, barring unexpected new information, this is a terrible look from OpenAI, as well as a large practical loss. Not that there is a great time to fire such people, but this was an extremely poor time.

随着更多信息的披露，情况看起来更糟了。

In the wake of the additional information, things look worse.

我不认为这是严格必需的，如果存在足够的沟通障碍，这取决于你如何看待被解雇的不完整解释。

I don't think this is strictly required, if there were sufficient communication breakdowns, depending on how you view incomplete explanations for being fired.

显而易见的替代假设是‘心怀不满的员工因被解雇而愤怒’，但 Kelsey Piper 说她认识三人中的两人，情况并非如此：

The obvious alternative hypothesis is 'disgruntled employees mad over being fired' but Kelsey Piper says she knows two of the three and this is not like that:

许多人称这三人为吹哨人。鉴于我并未听到任何哨声，也没有听到任何人声称自己吹过哨，我们无从得知这是否属实？但这一模式无疑暗示着某种隐忧。

There are many calling these three whistleblowers. We do not know that this is true, given I do not hear any blown whistles or claims to having blown one? But certainly the pattern suggests a concern.

狗在夜里什么也没做 The Dog Did Nothing in the Nighttime

没有任何技术团队成员或高管公开谈论过此事。

No members of technical staff, or executives, have spoken about this in public.

更令人不安的是，面对一个可能产生寒蝉效应的事件，除 Dean Ball 之外，我持续关注的许多 OpenAI 人士几乎无人在 Twitter 上就几乎任何话题发声——当然也有例外。在我所见过的表态中，没有一条有任何争议性或冒进之处。

Far more troubling, in response to an event that has a potential chilling effect, there are exceptions but almost none of the many people I keep tabs on from OpenAI other than Dean Ball are saying anything on Twitter at all, about almost any subject. Of the statements I have seen, none are at all controversial or frisky.

我完全不怪 OpenAI 的员工。情况很复杂，有很多我们不知道的事。在事情于内部推进期间，公开保持沉默是非常合理的做法。但这沉默依然震耳欲聋。

I do not at all blame the OpenAI employees for this. The situation is complicated, with much we do not know. Public silence is a highly reasonable move while things play out internally. The silence is still deafening.

另一种理论（我并不赞同） Another Theory (That I Do Not Agree With)

我并不同意的观点，但确实存在：甚至有人，比如 Arjun Panickssery 和 charding，认为所报告的理由看起来像明显的借口，这是一种特性而非缺陷。在这种理论下，如果你给出一个看似合理的借口，人们可能会相信它，而这会破坏制造寒蝉效应的目的。

Opinions I disagree with but that are out there: There are even those, like Arjun Panickssery and charding, who think that the reported reasons looking like obvious pretexts is a feature rather than a bug. Under this theory, if you gave a plausible pretext, people might believe it, and that would defeat the purpose of creating a chilling effect.

鉴于所采取的行动，这些反应并不令人意外 The Reactions Are Not a Surprise Given the Actions

再次强调，这些反应对任何相关方来说都不应感到意外。这件事看起来一直都会显得相当糟糕。如果 OpenAI 有办法让它看起来不那么糟糕，并且能够分享出来，我们早就应该知道了。

Again, these reactions should not come as a surprise to anyone involved. This was always going to look pretty damn messed up. If OpenAI had a way to make it look not pretty damn messed up, that it was able to share, we would know about it by now.

我不认为被解雇的员工在欺骗我们。我看不出他们有什么理由这样做，而且如果他们在离职会议的事情上撒谎，OpenAI 有能力反驳这一点。如果这是正确的，那就意味着 OpenAI 至少在沟通上严重失职，无论是对员工还是对公众，即使我们在其他方面给予最大限度的宽容。允许这种明显是借口的解释成为员工被告知内容的说法，这本身就非常非常糟糕，无论背后的事实如何。

I do not believe the fired employees are lying to us. I see no reason for them to be doing that, and if they are lying about the exit meetings then OpenAI has the ability to contradict this. If that is correct it means that OpenAI at minimum massively dropped the ball on its communications, both to the employees and to the public, even if we are otherwise maximally charitable. Allowing such obviously pretextual explanations to become the story of what the employees were told is just so, so bad on its own, no matter the underlying facts.

这很糟糕，但并非最糟糕 This Is Terrible But Not Maximally Terrible

我确实认为许多人都跳到了“最混乱”的解释上，而我觉得这不太可能为真，因为其中涉及激励因素。

I do think many are jumping to a ‘maximally messed up’ interpretation that I find unlikely to be true, because of the incentives involved.

以下是一些让情况不至于最糟糕的因素：

Here are some things that make the situation less than maximally terrible:

1. 我相信 OpenAI 解雇这些员工并非因为他们公开或内部表达了对安全的担忧。

1. I believe that OpenAI did NOT fire these employees due to their publicly or internally expressed concerns about safety.

2. 我相信 OpenAI 解雇这些员工并非因为他们倡导第三方承诺或第三方审计员或嵌入式评估员。

2. I believe that OpenAI did NOT fire these employees due to their advocacy of 3P commitments or third party auditors or embedded evaluators.

3. 我相信 OpenAI 解雇这些员工并非为了制造寒蝉效应或减少员工与第三方的合作。

3. I believe that OpenAI did NOT fire these employees in order to create a chilling effect or reduce employee cooperation with third parties.

这仍然让我们得不到一个好的解释，而且很可能永远得不到，同时那三名员工声称——我相信他们是真诚的——他们因倡导安全而被解雇。鉴于三人长期且良好的过往记录，这充其量是一个巨大的非受迫性失误，即便 OpenAI 出于某种原因被迫在这件事上认栽。而这又加入了长长一串彼此押韵的事件之列。

That still leaves us without a good explanation, probably permanently, and with the three employees claiming, I believe sincerely, that they were fired for their safety advocacy. Given the long and positive track record of all three of them, this was at best a huge unforced error, even if OpenAI was for whatever reason backed into taking an L on this. And it goes into a long line of incidents that rhyme.
