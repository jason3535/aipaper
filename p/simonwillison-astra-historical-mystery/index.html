摘要 · Abstract

本文报道了一个案例研究：历史学教授 Mark Humphries 使用 GPT 6.2 Astra 识别出耶鲁大学贝内克图书馆所藏一本 1808 年匿名速写本的创作者。在极少的提示下，该系统利用公开的一手和二手资料自主工作了 22 分钟，找到了一份鲜为人知的纸质档案，并预测：若查阅沙博子爵（vicomte de Chabot）的旅行日记，其中关于一次拜访的记载——日期比速写本中某处附近河流的日期早五天——将能证实其假设；结果确实如此，而这份资料本身对加拿大史和毛皮贸易史研究者而言也是一项重要发现。核心论点是：历史学家可能面临一种较温和版本的“清算”，类似于数学界因“深蓝”而经历的那场冲击；因为历史研究涉及不断的重新诠释，而非单一普世真理，且更接近哲学与文学，所以人类作者身份依然不可或缺。

This article reports a case study in which history professor Mark Humphries used GPT 6.2 Astra to identify the creator of an anonymous 1808 sketchbook held at Yale's Beinecke Library. With minimal prompting, the system worked autonomously for 22 minutes using publicly available primary and secondary sources, locating a little-known paper archive and predicting that a diary reference to a visit by the vicomte de Chabot, dated five days before a date in the sketchbook at a nearby river, would confirm its hypothesis if Chabot's travel journal were examined; it did, and the source is itself a significant find for Canadian and fur trade historians. The core argument is that historians may face a milder version of the reckoning mathematics experienced with Deep Blue, since historical scholarship involves constant reinterpretation rather than a single universal truth and remains closer to philosophy and literature, so human authorship stays essential.

局限 · Limitations

  • 单一案例研究无法确立智能体式史学研究的普遍可靠性。
    A single case study cannot establish general reliability of agentic historical research.
  • 成功依赖数字化档案与可验证预测，限制了在文献匮乏议题上的适用性。
    Success depended on digitized archives and a verifiable prediction, limiting applicability to poorly documented topics.
  • 自主溯源带来归属与核验负担，史学家仍须逐一审查。
    Autonomous source discovery raises attribution and verification burdens historians must still audit.
  • “较温和冲击”之说属推测，未在整个学科范围内实证检验。
    The claim of a milder reckoning is speculative, not empirically tested across the discipline.
  • 依赖专有前沿模型，给学术研究带来可复现性与可及性隐忧。
    Reliance on proprietary frontier models raises reproducibility and access concerns for scholarship.

**GPT 6.2 Astra Solves a Historical Mystery**. Here's an interesting case-study in using GPT-6.2 Astra for historical research, from history professor Mark Humphries:

仅需极少的提示，GPT-6.2 Astra 便能自主识别出耶鲁大学贝内克图书馆所藏一本 1808 年匿名速写本的创作者。它完成得极快（仅用 22 分钟），使用的是公开可获取的一手和二手资料。为此，它在一份论文档案中发现了一个鲜为人知的来源，预测若加以查验该来源将能证明其假设，最终确实如此。不仅如此，这一新来源本身还是一项重大发现，将对加拿大史和毛皮贸易史学者具有重要价值。

With only minimal prompting, GPT-6.2 Astra was able to autonomously identify the creator of an anonymous 1808 sketchbook held at Yale's Beinecke Library. It did so extremely quickly (in just 22 minutes), using publicly available primary and secondary sources. To do so, it discovered a little known source in a paper archive, predicted that it would prove its hypothesis if examined and, in the end, it did. Not only that, but this new source turns out to be a major find on its own that will be of significant interest to both Canadian and fur trade historians.

Astra 找到了一则日记记载，提到德沙博子爵（vicomte de Chabot）的一次到访，日期比速写本中提到的附近某条河流的日期早五天，并推测沙博的旅行日记（只能向克利夫兰州立大学订购获取）可能证实这本速写本出自其手。

Astra found a diary reference to a visit by the vicomte de Chabot dated five days before a date mentioned in the sketchbook at a nearby river, and suggested that Chabot's journal of his travels (only available by order from Cleveland State University) might confirm that the sketchbook was his work.

历史学家是否该迎来一轮“深蓝时刻”？Mark 认为影响会是温和的：

Are historians due for a round of Deep Blue? Mark thinks it will be mild:

但若以数学领域的经验为鉴，我们同样可以预见，明年春季或夏季的某个时候，我们将面临某种信心危机——我们将难以理解如何回应那些能做人文学者做不到之事的智能体式系统。平心而论，这场危机对我们而言几乎肯定不如对数学领域那样深刻。当然，数学的问题在于，解决困难的数学问题本身就是全部。而历史学者则不同，他们不断依据演变中的当下重新阐释过去；我们不追求单一的普遍真理。我们（至少在理想情况下）所写的书籍和文章，也更接近哲学与文学作品，而非科学论著。因此，人性在某种程度上是历史文献生产的必要条件。

But if the experience of mathematics is any guide, we can also anticipate that sometime next spring or summer we'll face a sort of crisis of confidence as we struggle to understand how to respond to agentic systems that can do things human historians cannot. To be fair, that crisis will almost certainly be less profound for us than it is for math. The problem in mathematics, of course, is that solving hard math problems was the whole game. Historians, on the other hand, constantly reinterpret the past in light of an evolving present; we don't chase a single universal truth. We also (in the ideal at least) write books and articles that are closer to works of philosophy and literature than they are to scientific tracts. Humanity is thus something of a requirement for the production of historical literature.
